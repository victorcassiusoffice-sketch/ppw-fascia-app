# Daily foundation

PPW Protocol · v1 · Evidence B

A few simple, evidence-first basics for most healthy adults.

**For:** Healthy adults who want a simple, evidence-first daily routine.

**Not for:** Pregnancy; Breastfeeding; Trying to get pregnant; Kidney disease (reduced kidney function)

**Review after:** 12 weeks

## The plan

1. **Get outdoor daylight** (15 min)
   30 min after waking · Every day · evidence C
   Daylight is the best source of bright light by day. It helps your body clock and sleep.
2. **Brisk walk** (30 min)
   15 min after lunch · Weekdays (Mon–Fri) · evidence A
   Aim for 150 minutes a week that makes you a bit out of breath. Any amount is better than none.
3. **Strength session** (20 min)
   17:30 · Every other day · evidence A
   Work all major muscles at least 2 days a week. Body weight or bands are fine. Skip a day if sore.
4. **Vitamin D3 10 µg (400 IU)** — Vitamin D 10 µg
   At breakfast · Every day · evidence B
   Take if you get little sun on your skin: autumn and winter, or all year if you are rarely outdoors or have dark skin.
5. **Omega-3 250 mg (fish or algae oil)** — Omega-3 (EPA + DHA) 250 mg
   At dinner · Every day · evidence C
   Only if you eat less than 1 portion of oily fish a week. Food first: 1 portion (140 g) of oily fish a week.
6. **Protein at every meal**
   At lunch · Every day · evidence B
   Aim for about 0.8 g of protein per kg body weight a day, spread over your meals.
7. **Fibre: aim for 30 g a day**
   At breakfast · Every day · evidence B
   Vegetables, fruit, beans, whole grains, nuts and seeds. Build up slowly over a few weeks.
8. **Wind down for sleep** (30 min)
   30 min before bedtime · Every day · evidence C
   Screens off 30 minutes before bed. Aim for 7 or more hours, at the same times each day.

## Safety

- Vitamin D: do not take more than 100 µg (4,000 IU) a day in total from all supplements.
- Check other products (like multivitamins) so you don't double up on vitamin D.
- Kidney disease or high calcium: ask your doctor before taking vitamin D.
- On blood thinners? Tell your doctor before starting fish oil.
- Heart rhythm problems such as atrial fibrillation? Ask your doctor first. Omega-3 supplements slightly raised this risk in trials, more so above 1 g a day.
- Pregnant, planning a pregnancy or breastfeeding? Ask your midwife or doctor for the right plan.
- New to exercise? Start small and build up slowly.

## Key studies

- World Health Organization 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour (2020, guideline). Adults should do 150-300 minutes of moderate activity a week plus strength work on 2 or more days. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33239350/)
- Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts (2022, meta-analysis). More daily steps were linked to lower risk of death, levelling off at 6,000-8,000 (age 60+) and 8,000-10,000 (under 60). [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35247352/)
- Carbohydrate quality and human health: a series of systematic reviews and meta-analyses (2019, meta-analysis). Eating 25-29 g of fibre a day was linked to the biggest drop in disease risk. Trials showed lower weight, blood pressure and cholesterol. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30638909/)
- Vitamin D for the Prevention of Disease: An Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline (2024, guideline). Advises against vitamin D above daily needs for healthy adults under 75, and against routine testing. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828931/)
- Omega-3 fatty acids for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease (2020, Cochrane review). Omega-3 supplements had little or no effect on deaths, with a small drop in heart disease events (low certainty). [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32114706/)
- Effect of Long-Term Marine Omega-3 Fatty Acids Supplementation on the Risk of Atrial Fibrillation (2021, meta-analysis). Omega-3 supplements were linked to more atrial fibrillation, especially at doses above 1 g a day. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34612056/)
