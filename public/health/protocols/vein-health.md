# Vein health

PPW Protocol · v1 · Evidence B · **Doctor first**

Calf-pump moves, leg rest and stockings, with doctor-guided vein supplements.

**For:** Adults with varicose veins or chronic venous insufficiency, alongside their doctor's care.

**Not for:** Pregnancy; Breastfeeding; Kidney disease (reduced kidney function); Heart disease; a hot, red, swollen or painful leg (see a doctor today); a leg ulcer or a bleeding vein; poor blood flow in the legs (for stockings)

**Review after:** 8 weeks

## The plan

1. **Calf pumps: heel raises** (3 min)
   10:00 · Every day · evidence C
   Rise onto your toes and lower slowly, 15-20 times. Hold a chair for balance. Repeat later in the day.
2. **Daily walk** (30 min)
   15 min after lunch · Every day · evidence C
   Regular walking helps blood flow in the legs. Build up slowly.
3. **Break from sitting or standing** (3 min)
   15:00 · Weekdays (Mon–Fri) · evidence C
   Avoid long spells of sitting or standing. Every 30 minutes, walk or do ankle pumps.
4. **Legs up** (15 min)
   30 min after dinner · Every day · evidence C
   Lie down with your legs raised on cushions.
5. **Put on compression stockings**
   15 min after waking · Every day · evidence B
   Only if a doctor or nurse has advised and fitted them. Stockings of at least 15 mmHg ease aching and swelling.
6. **Diosmin + hesperidin (MPFF) 1000 mg** — Diosmin + hesperidin (MPFF) 1000 mg
   At breakfast · Every day · evidence B
   Doctor-first. Micronised diosmin 900 mg + hesperidin 100 mg once a day. Allow at least 4 weeks to judge it.
7. **Alternative: horse chestnut extract**
   08:00 · Once · evidence B
   Only if your doctor prefers it to MPFF: standardised seed extract, 50 mg aescin twice a day. Never take both.

## Safety

- Get medical help the same day for a hot, red, swollen or painful leg. It can be a blood clot (DVT).
- Call emergency services if leg pain or swelling comes with breathlessness or chest pain.
- If a varicose vein bleeds: lie down, raise the leg and press firmly on it. Then get urgent help.
- See your GP for pain, itching or swelling, a sore that has not healed in 2 weeks, or veins that get worse.
- Before stockings, have the blood flow in your legs checked, especially if you have diabetes or poor circulation.
- Vein supplements may ease heaviness and swelling. They are not shown to make varicose veins go away.
- Use only one vein supplement, chosen with your doctor. Stomach upset is the most common side effect.
- Horse chestnut extract: labels show the dose in different ways (older ones say 50 mg aescin, newer EU ones 21 mg). Ask your pharmacist.
- Horse chestnut: only standardised seed extract. Raw seeds, bark, flowers and leaves are poisonous.
- Horse chestnut is not advised in pregnancy, breastfeeding or under 18. With heart or kidney problems, ask your doctor.
- On blood thinners or planning surgery? Ask your doctor first. Supplements are often stopped 2 weeks before surgery.

## Key studies

- European Society for Vascular Surgery (ESVS) 2022 Clinical Practice Guidelines on the Management of Chronic Venous Disease of the Lower Limbs (2022, guideline). Recommends stockings (at least 15 mmHg) for symptoms, suggests exercise, and says venoactive drugs should be considered for symptoms and swelling. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35027279/)
- Phlebotonics for venous insufficiency (2020, Cochrane review). Vein drugs probably reduce leg swelling slightly, with little effect on quality of life and slightly more side effects. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33141449/)
- Efficacy of micronized purified flavonoid fraction on improving individual symptoms, signs and quality of life in patients with chronic venous disease (2018, meta-analysis). 7 placebo-controlled trials (1,692 people): MPFF eased leg pain, heaviness, cramps and swelling. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29385792/)
- The Effects of Different Dosages on Micronized Purified Flavonoid Fraction's Treatment of Lower Limb Chronic Venous Disease (2026, meta-analysis). 1000 mg once a day worked as well as 500 mg twice a day. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39066519/)
- Horse chestnut seed extract for chronic venous insufficiency (2012, Cochrane review). Horse chestnut extract eased leg pain and swelling short term. Larger trials are still needed. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23152216/)
- Physical exercise for the treatment of non-ulcerated chronic venous insufficiency (2023, Cochrane review). 5 small trials (146 people). Exercise may help calf pump and venous refilling, but certainty is very low. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37314059/)
