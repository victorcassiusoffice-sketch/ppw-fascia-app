# Plant-based essentials

PPW Protocol · v1 · Evidence B

The key nutrients to cover when you eat vegan or vegetarian.

**For:** Adults who eat a vegan or vegetarian diet.

**Not for:** Thyroid condition; Pregnancy; Breastfeeding; Trying to get pregnant; Kidney disease (reduced kidney function)

**Review after:** 12 weeks

## The plan

1. **Vitamin B12 10 µg** — Vitamin B12 10 µg
   At breakfast · Every day · evidence A
   Essential if you are vegan. At least 10 µg a day, or 2,000 µg once a week instead.
2. **Iodine 150 µg** — Iodine 150 µg
   At breakfast · Every day · evidence B
   Use a potassium iodide or iodate supplement, not kelp or seaweed. Skip if you have thyroid disease, unless your doctor agrees.
3. **Algae omega-3 250 mg (EPA+DHA)** — Omega-3 (EPA + DHA) 250 mg
   At dinner · Every day · evidence B
   Raises omega-3 levels in vegans. Not yet shown to prevent disease. Keep DHA under 1 g a day.
4. **Vitamin D 10 µg (400 IU)** — Vitamin D 10 µg
   At breakfast · Every day · evidence B
   Autumn and winter, or all year if you are rarely outdoors or have dark skin. Vegan D3 (from lichen) or D2.
5. **2-3 calcium-rich foods**
   At lunch · Every day · evidence B
   Calcium-set tofu, fortified plant milk or yogurt, kale, broccoli or tahini. If you can't, ask about a 500 mg supplement.
6. **Iron and zinc foods at meals**
   At dinner · Every day · evidence B
   Beans, lentils, tofu, whole grains, nuts and seeds. Add vitamin C foods to meals. Keep tea and coffee between meals.
7. **Blood test: B12, iron and vitamin D**
   09:00 · on day 7 · Once · evidence B
   Ask your doctor about a check and how often to repeat it. For B12 in vegans, special B12 tests (MMA or homocysteine) are more reliable.

## Safety

- Thyroid disease? Ask your doctor before taking iodine.
- Don't use kelp or seaweed supplements for iodine. The amount varies a lot and can be far too high.
- Iodine: stay under 600 µg a day in total.
- Pregnant, planning a pregnancy or breastfeeding? Your needs change. Ask your midwife or doctor.
- Only take iron or zinc supplements if a blood test or your doctor says you need them.
- Vitamin D: no more than 100 µg (4,000 IU) a day in total.
- Calcium supplements: 500 mg or less at a time. Had kidney stones? Ask first.
- On blood thinners? Ask before taking omega-3.
- A normal blood B12 result can be misleading in vegans. Keep taking B12 unless your doctor says otherwise.

## Key studies

- A systematic review and meta-analysis of functional vitamin B12 status among adult vegans (2024, meta-analysis). Vegans had lower B12 and more signs of B12 shortage. Those taking B12 supplements had better results on every marker. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39373282/)
- Systematic review and meta-analysis of iodine nutrition in modern vegan and vegetarian diets (2023, meta-analysis). Vegans had the lowest iodine intake and status, especially where salt is not iodised. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36912094/)
- Vegan multinutrient supplementation significantly increases Omega-3 index and 25-OH-vitamin D status (2026, RCT). In 72 vegans, algae omega-3 plus vitamin D for 4 months raised omega-3 and vitamin D levels. A double dose added no clear benefit. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42480243/)
- Scientific Opinion on the tolerable upper intake level for supplemental docosahexaenoic acid (2026, guideline). EFSA kept the safe level for DHA from supplements at 1 g a day for all adults. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41542352/)
- Scientific Opinion on the Tolerable Upper Intake Level of EPA, DHA and DPA (2012, guideline). EFSA: EPA and DHA together up to about 5 g a day raise no safety concerns for adults. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42016086/)
