# Blood sugar support

PPW Protocol · v1 · Evidence B · **Doctor first**

Daily habits that help keep blood sugar steadier, alongside your care.

**For:** Adults with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes, working with their doctor or diabetes team.

**Not for:** Type 1 diabetes; Pregnancy; Breastfeeding; Weight-loss surgery; trouble swallowing or a narrowed bowel (for psyllium)

**Review after:** 12 weeks

## The plan

1. **Walk after meals** (10 min)
   After meals · Every day · evidence A
   A 10-minute walk soon after each main meal lowers the rise in blood sugar after eating.
2. **Movement break from sitting** (3 min)
   10:30 · Every day · evidence B
   Every 20-30 minutes of sitting, get up and walk for 2-3 minutes.
3. **Strength session** (20 min)
   17:30 · Every other day · evidence A
   Strength work on at least 2 days a week, plus 150 minutes of moderate activity a week.
4. **Vegetables and protein first, carbs last**
   At lunch · Every day · evidence B
   Eat the vegetables and protein on your plate before the bread, rice, pasta or potatoes. Use this at lunch and dinner.
5. **Psyllium husk 5 g before breakfast** — Psyllium husk 5 g
   10 min before breakfast · Every day · evidence B
   Stir into a large glass of water (at least 150 ml) and drink straight away. Ask your pharmacist how to time it with your medicines.
6. **Psyllium husk 5 g before dinner** — Psyllium husk 5 g
   10 min before dinner · Every day · evidence B
   Stir into a large glass of water (at least 150 ml). Not right before bed. Start with one dose a day for the first week if you feel bloated.
7. **Sleep 7-8 hours**
   At bedtime · Every day · evidence C
   The lowest diabetes risk is seen with 7-8 hours of sleep. Keep regular sleep times.
8. **B12 blood test if you take metformin**
   09:00 · on day 7 · Once · evidence B
   Long-term metformin can lower vitamin B12. Ask your doctor about a test, and repeat checks if you have risk factors.
9. **Vitamin D and magnesium: ask first**
   09:00 · on day 7 · Once · evidence B
   Vitamin D trials in prediabetes found a small drop in diabetes risk. Ask your doctor before taking it. Take magnesium only if a test shows it is low.

## Safety

- Keep taking your diabetes medicines. This routine adds to your care. It does not replace it.
- Psyllium and these habits can lower blood sugar. Tell your doctor before starting, as your medicines may need adjusting.
- On insulin, gliclazide or glimepiride? Know the signs of a low (shaky, sweaty, hungry, confused) and carry a sugary drink on walks.
- Always take psyllium with plenty of water. Do not take it if you have trouble swallowing, a narrowed bowel or a sudden change in bowel habit.
- Keep psyllium 30-60 minutes apart from other medicines, as it can slow their absorption. Ask your pharmacist about timing.
- Berberine and chromium are doctor-only. They can push blood sugar too low with diabetes medicines.
- Stop exercise and get help if you have chest pain, faintness or unusual breathlessness.
- Vitamin D: no more than 100 µg (4,000 IU) a day in total unless your doctor advises it.

## Key studies

- Advice to walk after meals is more effective for lowering postprandial glycaemia in type 2 diabetes mellitus than advice that does not specify timing (2016, RCT). In 41 adults with type 2 diabetes, 10-minute walks after meals lowered post-meal blood sugar more than one daily walk. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27747394/)
- Nutrient intake order on metabolic outcomes in type 2 diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis (2026, meta-analysis). Eating carbs last lowered blood sugar 1 hour after meals and HbA1c a little (0.16%). [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41081830/)
- The impact of food order on postprandial glycaemic excursions in prediabetes (2019, RCT). In prediabetes, eating protein and vegetables before carbs cut the blood sugar peak by over 40%. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30101510/)
- Psyllium fiber improves glycemic control proportional to loss of glycemic control: a meta-analysis (2015, meta-analysis). Psyllium before meals lowered fasting glucose and HbA1c in type 2 diabetes, less in prediabetes, and not in people with normal blood sugar. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26561625/)
- Long-term Metformin Use and Vitamin B12 Deficiency in the Diabetes Prevention Program Outcomes Study (2016, RCT). Long-term metformin was linked to low B12. Authors advise considering routine B12 tests. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26900641/)
- Vitamin D and Risk for Type 2 Diabetes in People With Prediabetes: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Individual Participant Data From 3 Randomized Clinical Trials (2023, meta-analysis). Vitamin D lowered the risk of developing diabetes by 15% in people with prediabetes. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36745886/)
- Sleep duration and risk of type 2 diabetes: a meta-analysis of prospective studies (2015, meta-analysis). Observational: the lowest diabetes risk was seen with 7-8 hours of sleep a night. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25715415/)
