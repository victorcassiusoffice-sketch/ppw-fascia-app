# Gut and microbiome

PPW Protocol · v1 · Evidence B

More fibre, fermented foods and plant variety, built up slowly.

**For:** Adults who want to support gut health through food and daily habits.

**Not for:** Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis); Weakened immune system; Having cancer treatment; new or unexplained bowel symptoms (see your GP first); a narrowed bowel or past bowel blockage

**Review after:** 6 weeks

## The plan

1. **Add one fibre food**
   At breakfast · Every day · evidence B
   Oats, beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit, nuts or seeds. Build up over about 4 weeks towards 30 g a day.
2. **One fermented food**
   At lunch · Every day · evidence C
   Yogurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut or kombucha. Start with 1 serving and add more slowly if you feel well.
3. **Count your plants this week**
   18:00 · Once a week · evidence C
   Aim for variety: vegetables, fruit, beans, grains, nuts, seeds and herbs. Linked to a more varied gut in a large survey, not a trial.
4. **Drink water through the day**
   10:00 · Every day · evidence C
   Aim for 6-8 glasses of fluid a day. Water, tea and coffee all count.
5. **On antibiotics? Ask about a probiotic**
   09:00 · Once · evidence B
   Only while you take antibiotics: a tested strain (such as S. boulardii or L. rhamnosus GG), started within 2 days of the first dose, at a different time from the antibiotic. Ask your pharmacist.
6. **Wind or bloating? Go slower**
   18:00 · Once a week · evidence C
   Some wind or bloating is common when you add fermented foods or fibre. In a trial it settled with time.
7. **What the app can and can't see**
   09:00 · Once · evidence C
   Ticks track what you eat and do. The app cannot measure your gut bacteria, and people respond differently.

## Safety

- See your GP if you notice blood in your poo, a lasting change in bowel habit, tummy pain, a lump, or weight loss without trying.
- Get urgent advice for bloody diarrhoea, diarrhoea lasting more than 7 days, or signs of dehydration.
- Do not take probiotics if your immune system is weak or you are seriously ill, unless your doctor says so.
- Probiotic effects depend on the exact strain. Choose one tested in trials, such as S. boulardii or L. rhamnosus GG.
- Experts disagree: one US guideline supports some probiotics with antibiotics, another advises against them.
- IBS, IBD or coeliac disease? Ask your doctor or dietitian before big changes to fibre or fermented foods.
- If more fibre causes pain, not just mild wind, slow down and get advice.

## Key studies

- Gut-microbiota-targeted diets modulate human immune status (2021, RCT). In 36 adults over 17 weeks, more fermented foods raised gut microbe variety and lowered inflammation markers. High fibre did not raise variety in that time. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34256014/)
- Carbohydrate quality and human health: a series of systematic reviews and meta-analyses (2019, meta-analysis). Fibre intakes of 25-29 g a day were linked to lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30638909/)
- Probiotics for the prevention of Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhea in adults and children (2025, Cochrane review). During antibiotics, probiotics may cut C. difficile diarrhoea by half and antibiotic-linked diarrhoea by a third. Low certainty. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40931979/)
- Probiotics for the Prevention of Antibiotic-Associated Diarrhea in Outpatients-A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (2017, meta-analysis). 17 trials: diarrhoea in 8.0% with probiotics vs 17.7% without. No more side effects. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29023420/)
- AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines on the Role of Probiotics in the Management of Gastrointestinal Disorders (2020, guideline). Suggests specific strains to prevent C. difficile in people on antibiotics. Probiotics for IBS and IBD only within trials. [Link](https://guidelinecentral.com/guideline/41160)
