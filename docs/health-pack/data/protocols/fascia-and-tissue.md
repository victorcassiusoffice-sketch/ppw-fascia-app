# Fascia and tissue support

PPW Protocol · v1 · Evidence B

Load your tissues well, eat enough protein, and time collagen before sessions.

**For:** Active adults doing regular strength or movement sessions who want to support tendons and connective tissue.

**Not for:** Kidney disease (reduced kidney function); Kidney stones; a new, hot, swollen or worsening injury (get it checked first)

**Review after:** 12 weeks · **Length:** 12 weeks

## The plan

1. **Collagen 15 g + vitamin C 50 mg** — Collagen peptides 15 g, Vitamin C 50 mg
   60 min before your session · Every other day · evidence C
   About 1 hour before your session, on training days. Trials used 15-30 g with about 50 mg vitamin C. Tendon results are mixed.
2. **Strength or loading session** (40 min)
   18:00 · Every other day · evidence A
   Slow, controlled work for all major muscles, at least 2 days a week. Add load gradually.
3. **Protein through the day**
   At lunch · Every day · evidence A
   If you train, aim for about 1.2-1.6 g protein per kg body weight a day, over 3-4 meals. More gave no extra gain.
4. **Vitamin C foods**
   At lunch · Every day · evidence B
   Citrus, berries, kiwi, peppers or broccoli each day. Your body needs vitamin C to make collagen.
5. **Vitamin D3 10 µg (400 IU)** — Vitamin D 10 µg
   At breakfast · Every day · evidence B
   Only if you get little sun on your skin, for example in winter or if you are mostly indoors.
6. **Mobility or foam rolling** (10 min)
   60 min before bedtime · Every day · evidence B
   Gentle mobility or foam rolling improves range of motion for a short time, about as much as stretching.

## Safety

- Kidney disease? Ask your doctor before raising protein or taking collagen.
- Had kidney stones? Ask first. Gelatin raised urine oxalate in a study, and high-dose vitamin C can too.
- Collagen is not a complete protein. It does not replace protein from meals.
- Collagen and gelatin come from animals. Check the label if you avoid some animal foods.
- Sharp pain, swelling, or pain that gets worse with loading: stop and see a physio or doctor.
- Vitamin C: stay well below 2,000 mg a day.
- Vitamin D: no more than 100 µg (4,000 IU) a day in total from all supplements.
- No study has measured fascia directly. Tendon and connective-tissue studies are the closest evidence.

## Key studies

- Vitamin C-enriched gelatin supplementation before intermittent activity augments collagen synthesis (2017, RCT). In 8 men, 15 g gelatin with vitamin C 1 hour before exercise doubled a blood marker of collagen building. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27852613/)
- The Collagen Synthesis Response to an Acute Bout of Resistance Exercise Is Greater when Ingesting 30 g Hydrolyzed Collagen Compared with 15 g and 0 g (2024, RCT). In 10 trained men, 30 g collagen with 50 mg vitamin C before squats raised collagen building more than 15 g or none. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38007183/)
- The Effect of Specific Bioactive Collagen Peptides on Tendon Remodeling during 15 wk of Lower Body Resistance Training (2023, RCT). In 39 young men, 15 g collagen a day did not improve tendon changes from training more than placebo. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37436929/)
- Hydrolysed Collagen Supplementation Enhances Patellar Tendon Adaptations to 12 Weeks' Resistance Training in Middle-Aged Men (2025, RCT). In 20 middle-aged men, 30 g collagen with vitamin C after training gave bigger gains in tendon size and stiffness. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40100255/)
- Impact of Collagen Peptide Supplementation in Combination with Long-Term Physical Training: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis (2024, meta-analysis). 19 trials: collagen plus training gave small gains in lean mass, tendon size and strength. Low to very low certainty. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39060741/)
- A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains (2018, meta-analysis). 49 trials: extra protein boosted strength and muscle gains from training. No added gain above about 1.6 g/kg a day. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)
- Acute Effects of Foam Rolling on Range of Motion in Healthy Adults: A Systematic Review with Multilevel Meta-analysis (2020, meta-analysis). 26 trials: foam rolling improved range of motion short term, about the same as stretching. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31628662/)
